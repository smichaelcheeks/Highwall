# Professional Civic Institutions of Highwall

## General structure

Highwall's professional institutions do not share a single hierarchy. Some
have operational chains of command. Others consist largely of independent
professionals under an institutional head responsible for standards,
professional admission, coordination, facilities, and liaison with upper
government.

Professional institutions ordinarily operate with substantial autonomy. The
Arbiter coordinates among them, resolves disputes, and retains formal authority
to direct them. Professional Council constituencies may encompass several
related professions without placing those professions in one administrative
hierarchy, and constituencies do not necessarily correspond one-to-one with
institutions or professions.

## Market institution

Market wardens possess general authority within the market and are
substantially independent and self-governing. A warden may habitually work in a
particular portion or range across the market by preference, but this creates
no distinct territorial jurisdiction among wardens. Wardens resolve disputes
arising within the market.

The market's institutional head is responsible for the market broadly,
including its facilities and people, rather than only supervising wardens. The
head's selection method is not established.

## Safety and mobility institution

A broad institution concerned with keeping people physically safe and able to
move includes rescue, fire response, lifts and vertical mobility, and the
infirmary and ordinary medical care.

Rescue captains command field teams typically consisting of approximately five
to eight rescuers. Captains are responsible for their teams' training and
maintenance, including field equipment and permanent rescue anchors installed
in Highwall. Senior rescue captains continue to command their own teams, take
precedence over regular captains in field command, and direct where regular
captains deploy their teams.

Structural inspection and safety belong to the Order of Stone, not to this
institution. Water management is also separate; sanitation and waste removal
belong to the water institution. The detailed hierarchy and whether a distinct
head of rescue exists are not established.

## Civic administration and archives

General governmental administration forms a recognized professional
institution or umbrella including clerks, civic recordkeepers, administrative
staff, and the archives. The judicial profession is organizationally distinct
but belongs to this umbrella for professional Council representation.

The Arbiter, as First Steward, is technically the institutional head. The
civic-administration professional Councilor is nevertheless selected by direct
vote. Separation of that seat from the Arbiter's institutional leadership is
intended to limit the Arbiter's power.

## Scope boundary

The complete roster, many institutional names and head titles, admission
procedures, professional constituency organization, and detailed hierarchies
are not established.
