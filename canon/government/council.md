# Council of Highwall

## Authority boundary

It is established canon that the Council is a single deliberative and voting
body. Geographic and professional Councilors do not form separate chambers.
The remaining structure on this page is working canon.

## Membership and constituencies

Council membership comes from geographic and professional constituencies,
which need not have equal numbers of seats. A citizen steward has both forms of
representation. Council office requires Stewardship.

Geographic constituencies correspond to physical sections of Highwall. The
Council may split, combine, enlarge, or reduce them as the city changes while
retaining that physical-section principle. It has established principles
intended to prevent political manipulation of the boundaries. Equal population
among geographic constituencies is not an established requirement.

Professional Council constituencies need not correspond one-to-one with
individual professions or administrative hierarchies. The Council may create
or reorganize either form of constituency as Highwall grows or its professions
change.

## Selection and representation

Geographic Councilors are selected by direct vote based on residence.
Professional Councilors are selected by procedures appropriate to their
constituencies, which may include direct vote, vote by senior professionals, or
selection associated with professional leadership.

Admission to Stewardship requires admission through a recognized profession.
A steward who belongs to multiple professions must designate one professional
constituency for Council purposes. Changing that designation requires petition
through the same general kind of process used for professional recognition;
the detailed process is not established.

Non-citizen residents receive formal geographic representation but do not vote
for their geographic Councilors. Geographic Councilors are expected to
represent their entire constituencies. Non-citizens may participate in debate
and discussion without possessing the final vote. They do not formally belong
to professional Council constituencies, although an institution where a
non-citizen works may provide incidental professional representation.

Visitors have no formal Council representation. They receive some incidental
representation through the market institution and have little direct
governmental say.

## Terms, vacancies, and expulsion

Councilors serve two-year terms. Geographic and professional selections occur
in alternating years, and constituents have no recall mechanism.

A Councilor expecting to leave early is expected to name a preferred
replacement. The Arbiter formally appoints an interim replacement and is
ordinarily expected, but not required, to select that designate. Any different
appointee must meet the eligibility requirements for the same seat. A prompt
special selection using the constituency's ordinary procedure fills the
remainder of the term.

The Council may expel one of its members by the shared constitutional
supermajority. Expulsion creates a vacancy filled through the normal selection
process for that seat. The numerical threshold and detailed expulsion grounds
and procedure are not established.

## Legislative authority

The Council has primary responsibility for laws, governmental guidance,
taxation, and budgets. Council legislation requires neither Arbiter approval
nor an Arbiter veto. Individual Councilors may introduce legislation; outside
interests require a Councilor sponsor to place a proposal formally before the
Council. The Arbiter may also introduce legislation as Speaker.

The Arbiter has no ordinary Council vote but casts a tie-breaking vote when the
Council divides evenly. The Arbiter's other legislative influence is described
on the [office page](arbiter.md).
