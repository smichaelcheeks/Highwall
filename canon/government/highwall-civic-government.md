# Highwall Civic Government

## Authority boundary

Highwall's divided civic sovereignty is established canon. The allocation and
operation of that sovereignty described below are working canon unless a linked
office page identifies a narrower claim as established.

## Constitutional structure

[The Council](council.md) and [the Arbiter](arbiter.md) are the principal
institutions through which Highwall exercises its divided civic sovereignty.
The Council is probably the first branch among equals in theory. In practice,
the Arbiter can exercise substantial power through interpretation and
implementation of Council-enacted law.

The Council controls legislation, governmental guidance, taxation, and
budgets. The Arbiter controls ordinary executive administration and may direct
professional institutions, subject to Council law and approved budgets.
Professional institutions ordinarily retain substantial operational autonomy;
the Arbiter coordinates them and resolves disputes among them.

These arrangements create intentional constitutional tensions rather than
resolved contradictions. The Council's formal primacy coexists with the
Arbiter's practical interpretive power, and institutional autonomy coexists
with the Arbiter's formal power of direction.

## Precedent and procedural adaptation

Highwall's governmental procedures rely substantially on accumulated
experience and precedent. When hazards, failures, or institutional weaknesses
become apparent, Highwall tends to learn from them and develop procedures for
similar circumstances. This reactive development can make its institutions
highly capable where experience is deep, but does not guarantee preparedness
for circumstances outside that accumulated experience.

Emergency review is one explicit connection between experience and later
institutional development. Its possible effects on law, procedure,
preparedness, and institutional practice are described with
[Storm Marshal](storm-marshal.md).

## Oversight and institutional accommodation

The Council may require the Arbiter to appear, answer questions, provide
records, and account for governmental decisions. If it disagrees with an
Arbiter's interpretation, it may enact new or more explicit law intended to
correct or constrain that interpretation. The Arbiter may interpret the new
law in turn. The two institutions are ordinarily expected to reach a workable
accommodation; persistent serious disagreement may ultimately lead to use of
the Council's removal power.

## Ordinary adjudication

Institutional officials may make initial decisions within their own
jurisdictions. Contradictory decisions and appeals may be taken to a
magistrate. Magistrates form an appellate level, while the Arbiter as Chief
Magistrate decides which magistrate decisions to review and which to allow to
stand. Detailed courts, crimes, penalties, enforcement, appeals, and
arbitration procedure are not established here.

## External governmental functions

The Arbiter personally holds Highwall's ordinary diplomatic authority, while
much routine work is handled through a foreign-affairs advisor and market
representatives. The Arbiter may negotiate treaties and new external
obligations, but Council approval is required before Highwall is formally
bound. The Arbiter possesses some implied diplomatic discretion, although its
boundaries are not established. Existing regional and imperial obligations are
primarily administered and coordinated by the Arbiter, subject to Council law
and budgets.

The Arbiter's regional arbitration function is more insulated from Council
oversight than ordinary civic functions. The Arbiter is expected to disregard
the Council's preferred outcome so that regional arbitration remains actually
and apparently impartial. The Council nevertheless retains its general
supermajority removal power and may remove an Arbiter for any reason sufficient
to persuade the required supermajority that removal is warranted.

The wider political relationship and Highwall's official treaty language are
owned by [Regional Imperial Structure](regional-imperial-structure.md).

Highwall officially presents itself as a sovereign polity dealing
diplomatically with the overseas empire. Imperial obligations are formally
framed as obligations arising from a treaty compact, which takes precedence
over Highwall's other compacts and obligations. This institutional presentation
does not alter the objective setting truth that the wider region functions as
an imperial tributary province.

## Emergency government

Ordinary flood command belongs to the separately activated
[Storm Marshal](storm-marshal.md), not to the Arbiter. Lesser emergency
authority and bespoke transfers of emergency command are also documented on
that office page.
