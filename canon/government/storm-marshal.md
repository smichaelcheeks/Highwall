# Storm Marshal

## Authority boundary

The ordinary flood office, its activation, command authority, training, and
review standards are established canon. The comparison with ordinary Arbiter
government, lesser emergency authority, and bespoke activation described below
are working canon.

## Ordinary flood activation

Storm Marshal is not a permanent office held continuously by one person. A
senior professional is called into service for an individual flood. Prospective
Storm Marshals receive training and must serve a practice flood as deputy Storm
Marshal before they are expected to lead a response.

Council-established objective and measurable flood conditions activate the
ordinary Storm Marshal regime. Other objective and measurable conditions end
it.

## Flood command

An activated Storm Marshal has total authority over flood response, including
resources, chain of command, positioning, and other response decisions. The
office theoretically includes authority to sacrifice one section of Highwall
to save a larger portion of the city when conditions warrant.

This authority is institutionally separate from the Arbiter's ordinary
authority. The distinction rests partly on the emergency's immediacy: ordinary
Arbiter government is expected to proceed deliberately within law and
precedent, while a Storm Marshal must act rapidly when consultation and
precedent review are impractical.

Storm Marshal is currently the only specifically identified governmental title
established as descending from an earlier military role.

## After-action review

Storm Marshal decisions receive formal after-action review involving the
Council and Arbiter. A hearing requires the Storm Marshal to answer for the
decisions and creates an official record.

Formal consequences are reserved for decisions believed to have been made
maliciously or for personal benefit or vendetta. Good-faith decisions that
prove suboptimal in hindsight are treated as unfortunate outcomes and learning
opportunities, not grounds for formal punishment.

As a working-canon clarification, the review also examines what Highwall should
learn from the emergency. The Council may consider whether observed hazards,
failures, or response problems should change laws, procedures, preparedness,
or institutional practices. After-action review therefore serves institutional
learning as well as judging the conduct and decisions of the emergency
officeholder.

## Lesser emergency authority

Council law recognizes that an emergency may require extraordinary action
without meeting the automatic flood conditions. In those circumstances,
extraordinary authority belongs to the Arbiter rather than generally to
individual institutional heads. Its exact activation conditions and powers are
not established.

Use of that authority is subject to an after-action Council review modeled on
Storm Marshal review. Because the Arbiter is under examination, the Arbiter
does not participate as a reviewer. The Council may terminate an
Arbiter-declared emergency against the Arbiter's wishes. As with Storm Marshal
review, the Council also examines lessons for future law, procedure,
preparedness, and institutional practice.

## Bespoke activation

Instead of terminating an Arbiter-declared emergency, the Council may keep the
emergency active and transfer extraordinary command to a Storm Marshal. The
Council defines a bespoke range of authority and bespoke ending criteria.

The default officeholder is the next eligible person in the existing Storm
Marshal pool. The Council may select a particular person especially suited to
the emergency, but departure from normal pool order requires the same
constitutional supermajority used for removal of an Arbiter or ruling family
and expulsion of a Councilor. The requirement prevents an ordinary political
majority from granting extraordinary power to a favored candidate while
allowing a specially appropriate expert to be chosen with sufficiently broad
agreement.

The numerical threshold is not established.
