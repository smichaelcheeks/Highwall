# Arbiter of Highwall

## Authority boundary and title

The office is titled **Arbiter** regardless of the governmental function being
exercised. Its four roles are established canon: Arbiter, First Steward,
Speaker of the Council, and Chief Magistrate. The selection, powers, and
practices below are working canon except where explicitly identified as
established.

## Powers and duties

The Arbiter is Highwall's principal executive authority. As First Steward, the
Arbiter heads general civic administration. The Arbiter may personally direct
professional institutions but ordinarily allows substantial autonomy,
coordinates their work, and resolves disputes among them. Ordinary executive
action remains constrained by Council-enacted law and Council-approved budgets.

As Speaker of the Council, the Arbiter presides over proceedings, participates
in debate, and may introduce legislation. The Arbiter has no ordinary Council
vote or legislative veto, but casts the tie-breaking vote in an evenly divided
Council. During deliberation, the Arbiter commonly advises Councilors how
proposed language would be interpreted and implemented, exposing interpretive
disputes before enactment.

As Chief Magistrate, the Arbiter stands above the magistrates and decides which
magistrate decisions to review and which to allow to stand.

## Selection and ruling family

The Council selects the Arbiter from the recognized ruling family. Selection
is substantive rather than automatic inheritance, and direct succession from
parent to child is historically rare.

It is established canon that the Council may replace the recognized ruling
family if it fails to produce worthy candidates, that forced removal of an
Arbiter is constitutionally associated with removal of the ruling family, and
that removal meeting the required threshold cannot be contested. The numerical
supermajority is not established.

If the Council wants to remove one Arbiter while retaining the broader family,
it may remove the ruling family and subsequently recognize the same family
again. It may instead recognize only a particular branch or replace the family
entirely.

## Resignation and the Arbiter-Designate

The Arbiter may resign voluntarily, either before a successor is selected or
by signaling an intention to resign upon the successor's selection.

It is established canon that the serving Arbiter names the
Arbiter-Designate. The designate exercises the interim office until the Council
formally selects the next Arbiter and is expected during that interval to avoid
decisions with long-term implications.

As a working-canon clarification, the designate is expected to focus primarily
on day-to-day government and continuity, and largely to refrain from actions
with substantial or long-lasting consequences when those actions can
reasonably await selection of the next Arbiter.

Accumulated history and precedent guide this expected restraint; it is not
established as a fixed list of legally prohibited acts. The temporary
officeholder retains the authority necessary to maintain government while
using circumstance and precedent to distinguish caretaker administration from
decisions better left to a formally selected Arbiter.

The designate is formally a placeholder and in practice also serves as a
right-hand or apprentice. Designation lets the incumbent's preferred successor
learn the office, demonstrate fitness, and become visible to the Council, but
does not bind the Council's choice. The Council may consider how well the
designate exercised interim restraint. Because designation belongs to the
Arbiter, an Arbiter may change the designate as part of a resignation if a
different person should exercise the interim office.

## Accountability and boundaries

The Council's legislative, budgetary, oversight, and removal checks are
described in [Highwall Civic Government](highwall-civic-government.md). The
Arbiter's extraordinary authority outside the ordinary flood regime is
described with [Storm Marshal](storm-marshal.md).

The boundaries of implied diplomatic discretion and the Arbiter's lesser
emergency powers are not established.
