# Flora and Fauna of the Highwall Region

## Status and scope

This page is a working inventory of organisms intended to occupy the niches in
the [established regional ecology](highwall-region-ecology.md). Entries may be
refined, renamed, combined, or replaced while that broader structure remains
consistent. The inventory is not a finalized biological catalog.

The existence of established regional activities such as reed cultivation
does not promote this page's organism descriptions, biological identities, or
inventory entries. Those details retain this page's working status unless
separately established.

The region includes native and human-influenced organisms. Centuries of
settlement and management have expanded or contracted some ranges. Communities
cultivate or encourage useful organisms and discourage others near settlements.

## Plateau inventory

- Mesquite-like trees concentrated around springs, washes, and groundwater.
- Semi-arid grasses and shrubs.
- Rabbits and other small grazing mammals.
- Wolves.
- Raptors.
- Pollinating and pest insects.
- Reptiles adapted to dry environments.

Mesquite-like trees provide food, shade, and habitat in otherwise dry areas.
Raptors regulate small-mammal and reptile populations, while wolves occupy a
major terrestrial-predator role across portions of the plateau.

## Canyon inventory

- Cliff-nesting swifts that seasonally form large murmurations.
- Ravens and other corvids.
- Hawks, falcons, and other cliff-dwelling birds of prey.
- Bats in natural cavities.
- Spiders and other predatory arthropods.
- Small reptiles.
- Human-associated dogs.
- Raccoon-like opportunistic scavengers outside Highwall but largely excluded
  from the city through human management.
- Occasional mountain lions outside settled areas.

The swifts consume large numbers of flying insects, and bats provide nighttime
insect control. Mountain lions occupy the largest-predator niche in remote
canyon environments. Highwall's inhabited canyon has gradually shifted toward
wildlife tolerant of close human presence as nuisance and dangerous wildlife
have become less common within the city.

## Basin inventory

- Seasonal reeds and marsh vegetation associated with managed wetlands.
- Waterfowl.
- Amphibians where seasonal water persists.
- Pollinating insects.
- Grazing mammals.
- Predatory birds.

Seasonal wetlands provide breeding habitat for aquatic and semi-aquatic
wildlife. Marshes maintained through temporary dams are a longstanding feature
of portions of the basin.

## Coastal plain inventory

- Massive redwood-analogue conifer forests in the Stormlands.
- Permanent-river fish communities.
- Large grazing animals.
- Forest predators.
- Wetland birds.

## Permanent-river inventory

- Freshwater fish.
- Aquatic invertebrates.
- Waterfowl.
- Riparian vegetation.

Freshwater fish are associated primarily with permanent rivers rather than the
intermittent river through Highwall's canyon.

## Human-influenced organisms

Working examples include mesquite-like trees, managed-wetland reeds,
domesticated honeybees, chickens, and rabbits near agricultural settlements.
Highwall and neighboring communities keep domesticated bees for agricultural
pollination and honey. These bees support cultivated crops and native flowering
plants. Human cultivation and water management have expanded several useful
plants' distributions.

Raccoon-like canyon scavengers and large predators are discouraged or excluded
within inhabited areas.

## Disturbance

Regional disturbance processes are documented by the established
[regional ecology authority](highwall-region-ecology.md).
