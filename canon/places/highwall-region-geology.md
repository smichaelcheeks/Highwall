# Geology and Geomorphology of the Highwall Region

## Geological structure

The Highwall region is underlain by two major sandstone formations. Redstone is
the older and lower formation. Whitestone was deposited above it and forms the
uppermost layer and continuous resistant caprock of the regional plateau.
Redstone extends well below the present floor of [Highwall's](highwall.md)
canyon.

An ancient fault developed through both formations after their deposition. The
fault later became inactive, but its course determined the location of
Highwall's canyon.

## Rock formations

Approximately 250 meters of Whitestone are exposed in the walls of Highwall's
canyon. Whitestone is highly resistant to weathering and erosion and can
support large excavated chambers.

Approximately 550 meters of Redstone are exposed below it in the canyon walls,
with further Redstone beneath the canyon floor. Redstone is easier to excavate
and less resistant to weathering and erosion than Whitestone. It cannot support
excavated chambers on the same scale as Whitestone.

## Regional plateau and escarpments

An elevated sandstone plateau dominates the region. Although valleys and
smaller canyons extensively dissect its surface, the Whitestone cap remains
continuous throughout the region. Highwall occupies a local elevation maximum
on the plateau.

Downstream, the plateau ends at a major escarpment overlooking the coastal
plain. A smaller escarpment separates the surrounding plateau from the lower
floor of the inland basin beyond Highwall.

## Inland basin

The inland basin is enclosed and occupies part of the inland plateau beyond
Highwall. Mountains and canyons surround much of it. An ancient lake once
filled the entire basin before draining catastrophically millennia ago. Salt
flats left by that drainage cover much of the basin. A much smaller saline lake
remains in the present day and overflows roughly once per decade.

## Highwall canyon

Highwall's canyon descends approximately 800 meters below the surrounding
plateau. It cuts through the full thickness of the Whitestone and deep into the
underlying Redstone. It is the only known location in the region where erosion
has completely breached the Whitestone formation; no other known regional
canyon penetrates that caprock.

The catastrophic drainage of the ancient inland lake produced a megaflood that
followed the ancient fault, breached the Whitestone, and rapidly excavated
essentially the entire modern canyon into the Redstone. The modern intermittent
river inherited this canyon. Later erosion has continued to modify the landform
without fundamentally creating it.
