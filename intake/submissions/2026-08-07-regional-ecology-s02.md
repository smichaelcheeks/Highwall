# Regional Flora and Fauna

## Authority

- **Intended authority:** Working canon
- **Intended session mode:** Direct-integration
- **Author's instruction:** Record the currently established and proposed flora and fauna of the Highwall region as a working inventory. This seed documents the present design intent for species occupying established ecological niches while allowing future refinement or replacement.
- **Scope:** Plants, animals, and other organisms occurring within the Highwall region, their habitats, ecological roles, relationships with humans, and current design status.

## Setting truths

- The Highwall region supports a diverse community of native and human-influenced organisms distributed across the plateau, canyon, basin, coastal plain, and permanent river systems.
- Some species occur naturally within the region, while others have expanded or contracted their ranges due to centuries of human settlement and environmental management.
- Human communities intentionally cultivate or encourage certain useful species while discouraging others near settlements.
- The organisms listed below represent the current working inventory rather than a finalized biological catalog.

## People and groups

- Highwall and neighboring communities practice domesticated beekeeping to support agriculture and honey production.
- Human settlement has intentionally reduced nuisance and dangerous wildlife within the inhabited portions of Highwall's canyon.

## Places

### Plateau

**Working occupants**

- Mesquite-like trees concentrated around springs, washes, and groundwater.
- Semi-arid grasses and shrubs.
- Rabbits and other small grazing mammals.
- Wolves.
- Raptors.
- Pollinating insects.
- Pest insects.
- Reptiles adapted to dry environments.

### Canyon

**Working occupants**

- Cliff-nesting swifts that seasonally perform large murmurations.
- Ravens and other corvids.
- Hawks, falcons, and other cliff-dwelling birds of prey.
- Bats occupying natural cavities.
- Spiders and other predatory arthropods.
- Small reptiles.
- Human-associated dogs.
- Raccoon-like opportunistic scavengers, largely excluded from Highwall itself through human management but present elsewhere in the canyon.
- Occasional mountain lions outside settled areas.

### Basin

**Working occupants**

- Seasonal reeds and marsh vegetation associated with managed wetlands.
- Waterfowl.
- Amphibians where seasonal water persists.
- Pollinating insects.
- Grazing mammals.
- Predatory birds.

### Coastal Plain

**Working occupants**

- Massive redwood-analogue conifer forests in the Stormlands.
- Permanent river fish communities.
- Large grazing animals.
- Forest predators.
- Wetland birds.

### Permanent Rivers

**Working occupants**

- Freshwater fish.
- Aquatic invertebrates.
- Waterfowl.
- Riparian vegetation.

## History and chronology

- Human cultivation and water management have expanded the distribution of several useful plant species.
- Seasonal marshes maintained through temporary dams are a longstanding feature of portions of the basin.
- Wildlife communities within Highwall have gradually shifted toward species that tolerate close human presence, while troublesome species have become less common within the city itself.

## Culture, systems, and terminology

### Human-influenced species

Current working examples include:

- Mesquite-like trees.
- Seasonal reeds associated with managed wetlands.
- Domesticated honeybees.
- Chickens.
- Rabbits near agricultural settlements.

### Human-excluded or discouraged species

Current working examples include:

- Raccoon-like canyon scavengers within Highwall.
- Large predators within settled districts.

## Relationships and dependencies

- Mesquite-like trees provide food, shade, and habitat within otherwise dry environments.
- Domesticated bees support both cultivated crops and native flowering plants.
- Murmurating canyon swifts consume large numbers of flying insects.
- Bats provide additional nighttime insect control.
- Raptors regulate populations of small mammals and reptiles.
- Wolves serve as major terrestrial predators across portions of the plateau.
- Mountain lions occupy the largest predator niche in remote canyon environments.
- Freshwater fish are associated primarily with permanent rivers rather than the intermittent Dryrun.
- Seasonal wetlands provide breeding habitat for aquatic and semi-aquatic wildlife.
- Wildfire periodically renews fire-adapted ecosystems.
