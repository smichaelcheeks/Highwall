# Correction Addendum: Missing Remainder of Narrative Truth Section

The prior lore seed was unintentionally truncated during transmission. It ended
after the word “internal” in the first sentence under `### Objective setting
truth`.

The intended text from that point onward is reproduced below. This addendum
continues the original seed; it does not revise or supersede the text that was
successfully received.

### Objective setting truth

Each clan, city-state, and Highwall governs itself as an independent sovereign
polity in its internal affairs.

Collectively, however, the region functions as a tributary province of an
overseas empire.

The empire expects the region to supply ships, food, soldiers, and other
obligations.

No single polity independently fulfills these regional obligations. Instead,
each contributes according to its geographic and economic specialization.

From the imperial perspective, the upriver clans fall within Highwall's
provincial administration.

Highwall's primary responsibility within this arrangement is facilitating the
movement of specialized raw materials from the upriver clans to the downstream
regions, where those resources contribute to fulfilling imperial obligations.

### Official or institutional position

Within the region, Highwall, the upriver clans, and the downstream city-states
recognize one another as independent sovereign polities.

Their relationships are conducted through diplomacy, trade, treaties, and
longstanding agreements between neighboring states.

Official terminology emphasizes trade relationships, cooperation, and shared
obligations rather than tribute or subordination.

### Character knowledge

Not established within this seed.

### Reader knowledge

The existence and political role of the overseas empire are intentionally
obscured during the early portions of the story.

Readers initially encounter the region through the same civic language used by
its inhabitants, including terms such as “trading partners,” “trade
relationships,” and “shared obligations” rather than language emphasizing
imperial authority or tribute.

### Spoiler notes

Later discoveries reveal that the region's political relationship with the
overseas empire differs from the understanding presented through early
narrative and official civic terminology.
