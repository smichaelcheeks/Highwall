# Highwall Civic Government and Offices

## Authority

- **Intended authority:** Working canon
- **Intended session mode:** Direct-integration
- **Author’s instruction:** Establish Highwall’s civic governmental structure and offices without inventing details or expanding into reserved legal, cultural, military, economic, geographic, or narrative subjects. Preserve within this working-canon seed the elements separately identified by the author as established canon.
- **Scope:** Highwall’s governing bodies, civic offices, allocation of authority, office selection and tenure, oversight, ordinary administration, emergency authority, external governmental relationships, and explicitly supplied historical development.

## Setting truths

### Established canon

- Highwall is an internally autonomous sovereign polity.
- Collectively, Highwall’s region functions as a tributary province of an overseas empire.
- Highwall is not the region’s largest polity or strongest military power.
- Highwall’s influence rests substantially on stable institutions, legal traditions, engineering expertise, accumulated knowledge, and control of the only practical regional corridor.
- Highwall’s institutions are generally functional, intentionally designed, and staffed by capable people. They remain susceptible to blind spots, over-optimization, rare events, competing priorities, and changing circumstances.
- Civic sovereignty within Highwall is divided rather than residing absolutely in either the Council or the Arbiter.
- Highwall distinguishes visitors, long-term residents, and citizen stewards.
- Stewards possess additional privileges and civic obligations, though their detailed legal rights and duties are outside this seed.
- Stewardship, competence, reliability, preparation, public responsibility, continuity, and long-term thinking are respected civic values.
- Flood prediction, rescue operations, canyon engineering, archival preservation, logistics, and jurisprudence are defining bodies of civic expertise.
- Highwall is the principal interface between the downriver coastal-plain city-states and the upriver highland clans, but it is not the exclusive intermediary for every local interaction.
- Some civic offices retain titles inherited from earlier military roles whose functions became civilian and administrative.

### Working canon

- The Council and Arbiter are the principal institutions through which Highwall’s divided civic sovereignty is exercised.
- The Council is probably the first branch among equals in theory.
- In practice, the Arbiter can exercise substantial power through interpretation and implementation of Council-enacted law.
- The Arbiter combines executive, judicial, and legislative leadership functions without possessing the Council’s ordinary legislative vote.
- Professional civic institutions ordinarily operate with substantial autonomy. The Arbiter coordinates among them and resolves disputes, but retains formal authority to direct them.
- The Arbiter’s ordinary executive authority remains constrained by Council-enacted law and Council-approved budgets.

## People and groups

### The Council

#### Established canon

- The Council is a single deliberative and voting body.
- Its geographic and professional Councilors do not constitute separate chambers.

#### Working canon

- Council membership comes from two distinct forms of constituency: geographic and professional.
- Geographic and professional seats need not exist in equal numbers.
- The Council may create or reorganize constituencies as Highwall grows or its professions change.
- Geographic constituencies correspond to physical sections of Highwall.
- The Council has established principles intended to prevent political manipulation of geographic boundaries.
- The Council may split, combine, enlarge, or reduce geographic constituencies as it considers appropriate while retaining the physical-section principle.
- Equal population among geographic constituencies is not established as a requirement.
- Geographic Councilors are selected by direct vote based on residence.
- Professional Councilors are selected according to procedures appropriate to their constituencies. These procedures may include direct vote, vote by senior professionals, or selection associated with professional leadership.
- Council office requires Stewardship.
- Councilors serve two-year terms.
- Geographic and professional selections occur in alternating years.
- Constituents have no recall mechanism.
- The Council may expel one of its own members by the same still-undefined constitutional supermajority used for removal of an Arbiter or ruling family.
- Expulsion creates a vacancy to be filled through the normal selection process appropriate to that seat.

### Representation

#### Working canon

- A citizen steward has both geographic and professional Council representation.
- Admission to Stewardship requires admission through a recognized profession.
- A steward may belong to multiple professions but must designate one professional constituency for Council purposes.
- Changing the designated professional constituency requires petition through the same general kind of professional-admission process used for recognition by a profession.
- The details of professional admission and petition are not established.
- Non-citizen residents receive formal geographic representation.
- Non-citizen residents do not vote for their geographic representatives.
- Geographic Councilors are expected to represent their entire constituencies, including non-citizen residents.
- Non-citizens may participate in debate and discussion but do not possess the final vote.
- Non-citizen residents do not formally belong to professional Council constituencies.
- A non-citizen may nevertheless receive incidental professional representation through the institution in which that person works.
- Visitors possess no formal Council representation.
- Visitors receive some incidental representation through the market institution but have little direct governmental say.

### Professional institutions

#### Working canon

- Highwall’s professional institutions do not all use the same internal hierarchy.
- Some institutions possess operational chains of command.
- Others consist substantially of independent professionals under an institutional head whose functions include standards, professional admission, coordination, facilities, and liaison with upper government.
- Professional Council constituencies do not necessarily correspond one-to-one with individual professions or administrative hierarchies.

### Market institution

#### Working canon

- Market wardens possess general authority within the market and are substantially independent and self-governing.
- Individual wardens may habitually work particular portions of the market or range across it according to personal preference; this does not create distinct territorial jurisdictions among wardens.
- Market wardens resolve disputes arising within the market.
- The market’s institutional head is responsible for the market broadly, including its facilities and people, rather than merely supervising the wardens.

### Safety and mobility institution

#### Working canon

- A broad civic institution concerned with keeping people physically safe and able to move encompasses rescue, fire response, lifts and vertical mobility, and the infirmary and ordinary medical care.
- Rescue captains command field teams typically consisting of approximately five to eight rescuers.
- Rescue captains are responsible for their teams’ training and maintenance duties.
- These maintenance duties include field equipment and permanent rescue anchors installed in Highwall.
- Senior rescue captains continue to command their own teams but take precedence over regular captains in field command.
- Senior captains direct where regular captains deploy their teams.
- Structural inspection and structural safety do not belong to this institution; they belong to the Order of Stone.
- Water management is institutionally separate.
- Sanitation and waste removal belong to the water institution.

### Civic administration and archives

#### Working canon

- General governmental administration constitutes a recognized professional institution or umbrella.
- This umbrella includes government clerks, civic recordkeepers, administrative staff, and the archives.
- The judicial profession is organizationally distinct but belongs to the broader civic-administration umbrella for purposes of professional Council representation.
- The Arbiter is technically the institutional head of this umbrella in the capacity of First Steward.
- The civic-administration professional Councilor is nevertheless chosen by direct vote.
- Separating this Council seat from the Arbiter’s institutional leadership is intended to limit the Arbiter’s power.

## History and chronology

### Working canon

- The basic Council–Arbiter governmental system predates the collapse of the Old Wall.
- The institution of a Council-recognized ruling family also predates the Old Wall’s collapse.
- The titles and functions of Arbiter, First Steward, Speaker of the Council, and Chief Magistrate are inherited from the pre-collapse government.
- The collapse of the Old Wall did not create a wholly new constitutional order.
- After the Old Wall collapsed, the then-Arbiter resigned.
- Following that resignation, the Council moved to remove the existing ruling family entirely.
- The Salverns became the recognized ruling family in the aftermath.
- It is historically rare for the Arbitership to pass directly from parent to child.

## Culture, systems, and terminology

### The Arbiter

#### Established canon

- The office is titled **Arbiter** regardless of which of its governmental functions is being exercised.
- The Arbiter’s four established roles are:
  - Arbiter;
  - First Steward;
  - Speaker of the Council;
  - Chief Magistrate.

#### Working canon

- The Arbiter functions as Highwall’s principal executive authority.
- As First Steward, the Arbiter heads general civic administration.
- As Speaker of the Council, the Arbiter presides over Council proceedings, participates in debate, and may introduce legislation.
- As Chief Magistrate, the Arbiter stands above the magistrates and may decide which lower decisions to review and which to allow to stand.
- The Arbiter has no ordinary Council vote.
- If the Council is evenly divided, the Arbiter casts the tie-breaking vote.
- The Arbiter may formally introduce legislation.
- In practice, the Arbiter advises Councilors during legislative deliberation regarding how proposed language would be interpreted and implemented.
- This practice helps identify disputes over interpretation before legislation is enacted.

### Council legislative authority

#### Working canon

- The Council has primary responsibility for laws, governmental guidance, taxation, and budgets.
- Detailed taxation, finance, and budgeting mechanisms are not established in this seed.
- Council legislation does not require Arbiter approval.
- The Arbiter possesses no ordinary legislative veto.
- Individual Councilors may introduce legislation.
- Outside interests must obtain a Councilor sponsor to formally place a legislative proposal before the Council.
- The Arbiter may introduce legislation in the capacity of Speaker.
- The Arbiter may exercise considerable practical authority through interpretation and implementation of Council law.
- If the Council disagrees with an Arbiter’s interpretation, it may enact new or more explicit legislation intended to correct or constrain that interpretation.
- The Arbiter may subsequently interpret that legislation as well.
- Council and Arbiter are normally expected to reach a workable institutional accommodation.
- If disagreement becomes sufficiently serious and persistent, the Council’s removal authority provides an ultimate institutional check.

### Arbiter selection, succession, and removal

#### Established canon

- The Council may replace the recognized ruling family if it determines that the family has failed to produce worthy Arbiter candidates.
- Forced removal of an Arbiter is constitutionally associated with removal of the ruling family.
- If the Council meets the required threshold for removal, removal cannot be contested.
- The required supermajority has not been numerically established.

#### Working canon

- The Council selects the Arbiter from the recognized ruling family.
- The Council’s selection is substantive rather than automatic inheritance.
- If the Council wishes to remove an individual Arbiter while continuing the same broader family, it may remove the ruling family and subsequently recognize the same family again.
- The Council may instead recognize only a particular branch if it wishes to exclude another branch.
- The Council may also replace the ruling family entirely.
- The Arbiter may resign voluntarily.
- An Arbiter may signal an intention to resign upon selection of a successor or may resign before a successor has been selected.

### Arbiter-Designate

#### Established canon

- The serving Arbiter names the Arbiter-Designate.
- The Arbiter-Designate exercises the office during the interval required for the Council to formally select the next Arbiter.
- While exercising the interim office, the Arbiter-Designate is expected to refrain from decisions carrying long-term implications.

#### Working canon

- The Arbiter-Designate is formally a placeholder.
- In practice, the position also functions as a right-hand or apprentice role.
- Designation allows the incumbent Arbiter’s preferred successor to learn the office, demonstrate fitness, and become visible to the Council.
- The Council is not required to select the designate as the next Arbiter.
- The designate’s ability to exercise restraint during the interim period is one factor the Council may consider when deciding whether to select that person.
- Because the Arbiter controls designation, an Arbiter who wishes a different person to exercise the interim office upon resignation may change the designate as part of the resignation.

### Council vacancies

#### Working canon

- A Councilor expecting to leave office before the end of a term is expected to designate a preferred replacement.
- The Arbiter formally appoints the interim replacement and is ordinarily expected to choose the departing Councilor’s designate.
- The Arbiter may set aside that designation and appoint someone else.
- Any alternative appointee must satisfy the eligibility requirements for the same geographic or professional seat.
- A prompt special selection then fills the seat for the remainder of the term.
- The special selection uses the constituency’s ordinary selection procedure rather than necessarily a direct popular ballot.

### Magistrates

#### Working canon

- Magistrates function as an appellate level within Highwall’s judicial structure.
- Institutional officials may make initial decisions within their own jurisdictions.
- Market wardens, for example, both administer the market and resolve market disputes.
- Contradictory decisions or appeals from such decisions may be taken to a magistrate.
- The Arbiter, as Chief Magistrate, decides which magistrate decisions to review and which to allow to stand.

### Storm Marshal

#### Established canon

- Storm Marshal is not a permanent office held continuously by one person.
- A senior professional is called into service as Storm Marshal for an individual flood.
- Prospective Storm Marshals receive training.
- Before being expected to lead a flood response, a prospective Storm Marshal serves a practice flood as deputy Storm Marshal.
- Council-established objective and measurable flood conditions govern activation of the ordinary Storm Marshal regime.
- Other objective and measurable conditions govern its termination.
- When activated for a flood, the Storm Marshal possesses total authority over flood response.
- This includes control over resources, chain of command, positioning, and other response decisions.
- The Storm Marshal theoretically possesses authority to order one section of Highwall sacrificed to save a larger portion of the city if conditions warrant.
- Storm Marshal authority is institutionally separate from the Arbiter’s ordinary authority.
- Storm Marshal decisions are subject to formal after-action review involving the Council and Arbiter.
- A hearing is held in which the Storm Marshal answers for decisions made and an official record is created.
- Formal consequences are reserved for decisions believed to have been made maliciously or for personal benefit or vendetta.
- Good-faith decisions that prove suboptimal in hindsight are treated as unfortunate outcomes and learning opportunities rather than grounds for formal punishment.

#### Working canon

- The defining difference between ordinary Arbiter government and Storm Marshal command is partly the immediacy and non-deliberative character of emergency decisions.
- The Arbiter is ordinarily expected to proceed deliberately within law and precedent; the Storm Marshal must be able to act rapidly when consultation and precedent review are impractical.
- Storm Marshal is currently the only specifically established governmental title identified as deriving from an earlier military role.

### Lesser emergency authority

#### Working canon

- Council law recognizes that emergencies may require extraordinary action without satisfying the predefined conditions for automatic flood Storm Marshal authority.
- In such circumstances, extraordinary emergency authority belongs to the Arbiter rather than generally to individual institutional heads.
- The exact activation conditions and extraordinary powers of this authority are not established.
- The Arbiter’s use of emergency authority is subject to after-action Council review modeled on the review of a Storm Marshal.
- Because the Arbiter is the official under examination, the Arbiter does not participate as a reviewer.
- The Council may vote to terminate an Arbiter-declared emergency against the Arbiter’s wishes.

### Bespoke Storm Marshal activation

#### Working canon

- Rather than terminating an Arbiter-declared emergency, the Council may transfer extraordinary emergency command to a Storm Marshal while keeping the emergency active.
- For such an activation, the Council establishes a bespoke range of authority appropriate to the emergency.
- The Council also establishes bespoke ending criteria.
- The default person called into the office is the next eligible individual in the existing Storm Marshal pool.
- The Council may instead select a particular person considered especially appropriate to the emergency.
- Departing from the normal pool order requires the same constitutional supermajority used for Arbiter removal and Councilor expulsion.
- This requirement is intended to prevent an ordinary political majority from granting extraordinary powers to a favored candidate while allowing an especially appropriate expert to be selected when sufficiently broad agreement exists.

### Shared constitutional supermajority

#### Working canon

- The same constitutional supermajority threshold applies to:
  - removal of an Arbiter or ruling family;
  - expulsion of a Councilor;
  - selection of a bespoke Storm Marshal out of the ordinary pool order.
- The exact numerical threshold is not established.

## Relationships and dependencies

### Council and Arbiter

- **Established canon:** Civic sovereignty is divided.
- **Working canon:** The Council controls legislation, governmental guidance, taxation, and budgets.
- **Working canon:** The Arbiter controls ordinary executive administration and possesses substantial interpretive authority.
- **Working canon:** The Arbiter may personally direct professional institutions but ordinarily allows them to operate autonomously, coordinating their work and resolving disputes.
- **Working canon:** Direct Arbiter intervention remains constrained by Council law and budget.
- **Working canon:** Council oversight ordinarily includes the ability to require the Arbiter to appear, answer questions, provide records, and account for governmental decisions.

### Council and professional institutions

- **Working canon:** Professional institutions possess substantial operational autonomy.
- **Working canon:** Professional constituencies participate directly in Council representation.
- **Working canon:** A professional Council constituency may encompass multiple related professions without placing them within a single administrative hierarchy.

### Arbiter and regional arbitration

- **Working canon:** The Arbiter’s regional arbitration function is more insulated from Council oversight than the Arbiter’s ordinary civic functions.
- **Working canon:** The Arbiter is expected to decide regional arbitrations without considering the Council’s preferred outcome.
- **Working canon:** This insulation exists to protect both the actuality and appearance of impartial regional arbitration.
- **Working canon:** The Council nevertheless retains its general supermajority removal power and may remove an Arbiter for any reason sufficient to convince the required supermajority that removal is warranted.

### External political administration

- **Working canon:** The Arbiter personally holds Highwall’s ordinary diplomatic authority.
- **Working canon:** Much routine diplomatic activity is handled through a foreign-affairs advisor and market representatives.
- **Working canon:** The Arbiter may negotiate treaties and new external obligations, but Council approval is required before Highwall is formally bound.
- **Working canon:** The Arbiter possesses some implied diplomatic discretion, the boundaries of which are not established.
- **Working canon:** Administration and coordination of existing regional and imperial obligations are primarily executive responsibilities of the Arbiter.
- **Working canon:** Fulfillment of those obligations remains subject to Council law and budgets.

## Official or institutional positions

### Established canon

- Highwall officially presents regional political relationships through the language of trade, cooperation, diplomacy, treaties, and shared obligations rather than tribute or imperial subordination.

### Working canon

- Highwall officially presents itself as a sovereign polity dealing diplomatically with the overseas empire.
- Obligations to the empire are formally framed as obligations arising from a treaty compact.
- That treaty compact takes precedence over Highwall’s other compacts and obligations.
- This official presentation does not alter the objective setting truth that the wider region functions as an imperial tributary province.
- The regional Arbiter is institutionally expected to disregard the Council’s preferred outcome when conducting regional arbitration.

Highwall’s civic sovereignty is divided between institutions rather than vested absolutely in a single office or body.

The Arbiter exercises multiple governmental functions under a single title.

Regional arbitration is expected to remain independent of Highwall’s internal political preferences.

## Proposals and alternatives

- Tentative: the market’s institutional head may be nominated by the market wardens and confirmed by the Arbiter. This selection method has not been established.
- Possible but unconfirmed: a distinct head of rescue may exist between the senior rescue captains and the head of the broader safety and mobility institution.
- The exact constitutional supermajority was discussed as possibly being approximately three-quarters, but no numerical threshold has been established.

## Apparent contradictions

- The Council is probably the first branch among equals in theory, while the Arbiter may exercise greater practical power through creative interpretation and implementation of Council law. This tension is intentional and unresolved.
- The Arbiter’s regional arbitration function is expected to remain independent of Council political preferences, while the Council retains the power to remove the Arbiter if the required supermajority concludes removal is warranted.
- Professional institutions ordinarily operate autonomously, while the Arbiter retains formal authority to personally direct them.
- The Arbiter possesses no legislative veto and ordinarily has no Council vote, while still exercising significant legislative influence through the Speaker role, debate, legislative introduction, interpretive advice, and tie-breaking votes.
- Highwall officially treats its imperial relationship as a sovereign treaty compact while objectively participating in a tributary provincial arrangement.
