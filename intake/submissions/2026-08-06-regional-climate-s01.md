# Regional Climate

## Authority

- **Intended authority:** Established canon
- **Intended session mode:** Direct-integration
- **Author's instruction:** Establish the regional climate that drives Highwall's physical environment, seasonal rhythms, and hydrology while leaving unresolved meteorological details for future development.
- **Scope:** Regional climate, seasonal weather patterns, precipitation systems, climatic zones, storm season, and local microclimates.

## Setting truths

- The region has a predominantly semi-arid climate.
- The coast is significantly wetter than the plateau.
- The plateau lies within a rain shadow created by the coastal escarpment.
- Climate varies substantially with elevation and distance from the sea.
- Most annual precipitation arrives through two distinct seasonal moisture systems.
- Cool-season atmospheric rivers arriving from the western sea provide most winter precipitation.
- Late-summer and early-autumn moisture is transported northward from tropical waters south of the region, producing the seasonal storm cycle.
- Winter snowfall is an important source of regional freshwater.
- The eastern mountains accumulate substantial seasonal snowpack.
- Spring snowmelt is a major source of regional surface water.
- The eastern plateau experiences a more reliable late-summer storm season than the western plateau.
- Storms during late summer and early autumn are frequently localized.
- One watershed may receive heavy rainfall while neighboring watersheds receive little or none.
- Multiple flash floods occur during a typical year.
- Flash floods usually originate from storms occurring upstream rather than directly above Highwall.
- Annual precipitation varies considerably between years.
- Multi-year droughts occur.
- The north-south orientation of Highwall Canyon produces pronounced daily and seasonal cycles of sunlight and shade.
- The plateau surface and canyon rims are generally more exposed to wind than the canyon floor.
- The canyon interior is generally more sheltered from wind than the surrounding plateau.

## Places

### Stormlands

- Receive the greatest winter precipitation.
- Winter rainfall supports extensive forests.
- These forests support the region's timber resources and shipbuilding industry.

### Western Plateau

- Semi-arid.
- Receives substantially less precipitation than the coast because of the coastal rain shadow.

### Eastern Plateau

- Semi-arid.
- Experiences a more dependable late-summer storm season than the western plateau.

### Eastern Mountains

- Receive the greatest snowfall in the region.
- Act as the principal source of spring snowmelt.
- Promote late-summer thunderstorm development.

### Salt Basin

- Remains dry through much of the year.
- Storm season produces temporary freshwater runoff from surrounding mountains.
- Seasonal flooding around the basin margins creates temporary wetlands before conditions become dry again.

### Highwall Canyon

- The canyon's north-south orientation produces distinctive daily and seasonal lighting patterns.
- Conditions within the canyon may differ noticeably from those on the plateau above due to sheltering and topography.

## History and chronology

### Annual seasonal cycle

#### Winter

- Cool-season storm systems arrive from the western sea.
- The Stormlands receive frequent rainfall.
- As these systems cross the coastal escarpment they lose much of their moisture.
- The plateau therefore receives considerably less winter precipitation than the coast.
- The eastern mountains accumulate seasonal snow.
- Some winter precipitation reaches the plateau and Highwall, though snowfall on the canyon floor is uncommon.

#### Spring

- Snowmelt from the eastern mountains becomes an important regional water source.
- River flow becomes more dependable.
- Groundwater recharge increases.
- Spring represents the most reliable period of surface water availability.

#### Early Summer

- Temperatures rise.
- Much of the plateau dries.
- Travel conditions are generally favorable.
- Water becomes increasingly dependent upon springs, stored supplies, and remaining streamflow.

#### Late Summer and Early Autumn

- Heating over the interior draws warm, humid air northward from tropical waters south of the region.
- This marks the beginning of storm season.
- Thunderstorms become common across the eastern plateau, mountain front, and upper watersheds.
- Storms are frequently localized.
- Most flash floods originate from these storms.
- Flooding often occurs even when Highwall itself receives little or no rain.

#### Late Autumn

- Storm frequency gradually declines.
- Temperatures cool.
- Surface water transitions toward winter conditions.

## Culture, systems, and terminology

### Storm season

- Storm season is a defining feature of the region.
- It occurs during late summer and early autumn.
- Multiple flash floods are expected during a typical year.
- Storm season strongly influences travel, trade, seasonal labor, pastoral movement, Blueleaf harvests, reed cultivation, and flood preparedness throughout the region.

### Local microclimates

- The plateau surface and canyon rims are generally more exposed to wind than lower elevations.
- Wind conditions are an important practical consideration for travel, construction, and daily life.
- The canyon floor is generally more sheltered from wind than the exposed rim.
- Elevation, slope orientation, and exposure produce substantial local variation in snowfall, snow retention, moisture, and growing conditions.

- Late-summer storms produce the region's recurring flash floods.
- Variable precipitation contributes to recurring droughts.
- The coastal escarpment creates the plateau rain shadow.
- Mountain snowfall and storm formation influence downstream water availability.

### Objective setting truth

The region is governed by two primary seasonal moisture systems: winter atmospheric rivers arriving from the western sea and late-summer moisture transported northward from tropical waters south of the region. Together these systems create a semi-arid plateau, wetter coastal lowlands, dependable mountain snowpack, recurring seasonal flash floods, and periodic drought.
