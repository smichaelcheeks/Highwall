# Regional Ecology

## Authority

- **Intended authority:** Established canon
- **Intended session mode:** Direct-integration
- **Author's instruction:** Establish the broad ecological structure of the Highwall region, including its major ecosystems, ecological niches, seasonal dynamics, and human influence, without committing to specific species except where already established.
- **Scope:** Regional ecosystems, ecological functions, habitat relationships, environmental pressures, and long-term interactions between humans and the natural environment.

## Setting truths

- The Highwall region contains multiple distinct ecological zones, each supporting different communities of plants and animals.
- Ecological communities are shaped by elevation, water availability, seasonal flooding, winter snow accumulation, and the region's sandstone geology.
- Most annual biological productivity is driven by seasonal water availability rather than consistent year-round rainfall.
- Human settlement has significantly influenced portions of the regional ecology over many generations.
- Human management has expanded the range of some useful plants and animals while reducing or excluding others from settled areas.
- Wildfire is a recurring natural disturbance within appropriate ecosystems and contributes to long-term ecological renewal.
- Seasonal flood events periodically reshape canyon habitats and transport sediment, nutrients, seeds, and organic material downstream.

## Places

### Plateau

- The plateau supports semi-arid ecosystems adapted to seasonal precipitation, winter snow, and prolonged dry periods.
- Vegetation becomes denser around springs, seeps, washes, and other reliable water sources.
- Groundwater-supported woodland occurs where sufficient water reaches the surface.

### Canyon

- The canyon contains a distinct ecological community influenced by cliffs, intermittent water flow, shade, and exposed stone.
- Vertical rock faces provide habitat for cliff-adapted wildlife.
- Seasonal floodwaters periodically disturb portions of the canyon floor while leaving higher elevations comparatively stable.

### Basin

- The interior basin contains environments ranging from dry salt flats to seasonally productive wetlands.
- Temporary wetlands develop where seasonal water is retained.
- Some wetlands are maintained or expanded through deliberate human water management.

### Coastal Plain

- Permanent rivers and greater water availability support more productive ecosystems than the plateau and canyon.
- Extensive forests occur within appropriate portions of the coastal region.

## History and chronology

- Human communities have influenced regional ecosystems over many generations through cultivation, water management, animal husbandry, and settlement.
- Some modern ecological distributions reflect long-term human activity rather than entirely natural processes.

## Culture, systems, and terminology

- Managed landscapes form an established part of regional ecology rather than existing in opposition to it.
- Agricultural, grazing, and water-management practices intentionally shape portions of the environment while remaining dependent upon seasonal natural cycles.
- Domesticated pollinators are an established component of agriculture where appropriate.

## Relationships and dependencies

- Seasonal storms, winter snowpack, and spring snowmelt regulate annual ecological productivity.
- Intermittent rivers and permanent waterways support distinct ecological communities.
- Pollinators support both wild vegetation and cultivated crops.
- Herbivores, predators, scavengers, decomposers, and detritivores together maintain ecological balance throughout the region.
- Cliff ecosystems support species adapted to nesting, hunting, sheltering, or traveling within steep terrain.
- Aquatic ecosystems vary significantly between intermittent waterways and permanent rivers.
- Human settlements benefit from surrounding ecosystems while also modifying them through continual management.
- Some wildlife thrives alongside human settlements, while other species are actively discouraged or excluded from inhabited areas.
