---

Setting truths

The Highwall region is underlain by two major sandstone formations.

A resistant upper formation, known as Whitestone, forms the continuous caprock of the regional plateau.

A softer lower formation, known as Redstone, underlies the Whitestone and extends well below the present canyon floor.

An ancient fault crosses the region.

Highwall's canyon is the only known location where erosion has completely breached the Whitestone formation.


---

Places

Whitestone

Whitestone forms the uppermost layer of the regional plateau.

Approximately 250 meters of Whitestone are exposed along the walls of Highwall's canyon.

Whitestone is highly resistant to weathering and erosion.

Whitestone is capable of supporting large excavated chambers.

Redstone

Redstone lies beneath the Whitestone formation.

Approximately 550 meters of Redstone are exposed along the walls of Highwall's canyon.

Additional Redstone extends below the canyon floor.

Redstone is easier to excavate than Whitestone.

Redstone is less resistant to weathering and erosion than Whitestone.

Redstone cannot support excavated chambers of the same scale as Whitestone.

Plateau

The Highwall region is dominated by an elevated sandstone plateau capped by a continuous Whitestone formation.

Although extensively eroded into valleys and smaller canyons, the plateau retains its continuous Whitestone cap throughout the region.

Highwall occupies a local elevation maximum on the plateau.

Coastal Escarpment

The plateau terminates along a major escarpment overlooking the downstream coastal plain.

Inland Basin

An enclosed basin occupies part of the inland plateau beyond Highwall.

The basin floor lies below the surrounding plateau and is separated from it by a smaller escarpment.

Much of the basin consists of salt flats left behind after the catastrophic drainage of an ancient inland lake.

A saline lake remains within the basin and periodically overflows.

Mountains and canyons surround much of the basin.

Highwall Canyon

Highwall's canyon cuts completely through the Whitestone formation and extends deep into the underlying Redstone.

The canyon extends approximately 800 meters below the surrounding plateau.

No other known canyon within the region penetrates through the Whitestone formation.


---

History and chronology

The Redstone formation predates the Whitestone formation.

Following deposition of both formations, a fault developed through the rock sequence.

The fault later became inactive.

An ancient inland lake occupied the present inland basin.

The inland lake eventually drained catastrophically.

The resulting megaflood followed the ancient fault and rapidly excavated essentially the entire modern Highwall canyon.

The modern intermittent river occupies the canyon created by this earlier event.

Subsequent erosion has continued to modify the canyon without fundamentally creating it.


---

Relationships and dependencies

Whitestone forms the resistant caprock that preserves the regional plateau.

Redstone underlies the Whitestone throughout the Highwall region.

The ancient fault determined the location of the Highwall canyon.

The catastrophic drainage of the inland lake breached the Whitestone formation and excavated the canyon into the underlying Redstone.

The modern river inherited the canyon created by the megaflood.
