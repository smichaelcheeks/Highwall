# Highwall Design Principles

These principles guide authors and AI collaborators. They are authoritative
creative policy, not evidence for setting facts. The factual premise about
Highwall's environment and civilization belongs in
[`Highwall`](../canon/places/highwall.md).

## Competent, imperfect people

Portray people as capable but fallible. Important decisions should be reasonable
given the decision-maker's priorities, limitations, and available information,
even when the result is mistaken or harmful.

## Conflict without simple villains

Prefer conflict arising from competing priorities, incentives, interpretations,
uncertainty, institutional constraints, and chance over conflict requiring
unambiguous heroes and villains.

## Environmental causality

When extending the setting, examine how sustained environmental pressure could
shape institutions, culture, law, religion, engineering, economy, and civic
values. Preserve credible interaction among these systems rather than treating
them as arbitrary decorations.

This principle does not establish any particular system or causal history. Such
claims require their own canon authority.

## Institutional competence

Begin from institutions that are functional, intentionally designed, and
usually staffed by capable people. Let their limitations arise from blind spots,
over-optimization, rare events, competing objectives, and changing conditions
rather than default incompetence.

## Stewardship and continuity

Emphasize stewardship, accumulated knowledge, continuity, and long-term
thinking. Avoid making individual exceptionalism the sole source of social
competence, preservation, or progress.

## Earned causality

Reward careful observation and historical context. Avoid using convenient
coincidence as the primary resolution of an important problem.
